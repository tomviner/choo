How do users navigate between views?

Each option shows the same scenario: you want trains from Highbury to Moorgate

A

Explicit Commands

User picks the view they want upfront

$ tt next home work

  Highbury & Islington → Moorgate
  ❶ 18:42  Plat 2  On time   (6 min)
  ❷ 18:49  Plat 2  On time   (13 min)
  ❸ 18:57  Plat 1  +2 min   (21 min)

$ tt board home

  Highbury & Islington — Departures
  TIME   DEST          PLAT  STATUS     OPERATOR
  18:42  Moorgate       2    On time    Northern
  18:45  New Barnet     1    On time    Northern
  18:49  Moorgate       2    On time    Northern
  18:52  Welwyn G.C.    1    +3 min     Northern
  18:57  Moorgate       1    +2 min     Northern
  ...

$ tt service G54821

  G54821 — Northern — 14 Mar 2026
  High Barnet → Moorgate

  High Barnet      dep 18:20  18:20
  Totteridge       dep 18:24  18:24
  Woodside Park    dep 18:27  18:27
  ...
  Highbury & Isl.  dep 18:42  18:42  ◀ AT PLATFORM
  Essex Road       dep 18:44
  Old Street       dep 18:47
  Moorgate         arr 18:49

Pros

  • Predictable — you get what you ask for
  • Easy to document
  • Scriptable — each command is self-contained

Cons

  • User must know all 3 commands
  • Drilling into a service means copy-pasting the UID
B

Interactive Drill-Down

Start with a board, use arrow keys / numbers to drill into a service

$ tt home work

  Highbury & Islington → Moorgate
  ───────────────────────────────────
  ▸ 18:42  Plat 2  On time   (6 min)
    18:49  Plat 2  On time   (13 min)
    18:57  Plat 1  +2 min   (21 min)
    19:04  Plat 2  On time   (28 min)
  ───────────────────────────────────
  ↑↓ navigate  enter: details  q: quit

  [user presses Enter on 18:42]

  G54821 — Northern — 14 Mar 2026
  High Barnet → Moorgate

  High Barnet      dep 18:20  18:20
  Totteridge       dep 18:24  18:24
  ...
  Highbury & Isl.  dep 18:42  18:42  ◀ AT PLATFORM
  Old Street       dep 18:47
  Moorgate         arr 18:49
  ───────────────────────────────────
  esc: back to list  q: quit

Pros

  • Seamless — no UID copy-paste
  • Discoverable — one command does everything
  • Feels like a real app

Cons

  • Not scriptable / pipeable
  • Harder to implement (TUI)
  • Can't use in non-interactive contexts
C

Smart Default + Flags

One command, output adapts. Flags to change view. Numbered rows for drill-down.

# Two stations? You get "next 3"
$ tt home work

  Highbury & Islington → Moorgate
  [1] 18:42  Plat 2  On time   (6 min)
  [2] 18:49  Plat 2  On time   (13 min)
  [3] 18:57  Plat 1  +2 min   (21 min)

# One station? You get the board
$ tt home

  Highbury & Islington — Departures
  [1] 18:42  Moorgate       Plat 2  On time
  [2] 18:45  New Barnet     Plat 1  On time
  [3] 18:49  Moorgate       Plat 2  On time
  [4] 18:52  Welwyn G.C.    Plat 1  +3 min
  ...

# Drill into row [1] by number
$ tt home 1
# or by service UID directly
$ tt service G54821

  G54821 — Northern — 14 Mar 2026
  ...full calling pattern...

# Override density
$ tt home work --all      # force board view
$ tt home --arrivals      # arrival board
$ tt home work --at 14:30 # next 3 around 2:30pm

Pros

  • Minimal typing — smart about what you want
  • Numbered rows enable "tt home 1" drill-down
  • Still scriptable (non-interactive)
  • One mental model: "tt [from] [to?]"

Cons

  • "tt home 1" needs state — remembers last query
  • Magic behaviour needs good --help
  • Row numbers could conflict with other args
